    <h2 id="overview-title">A bigger role in<br />what comes next.</h2>
    <p class="school-lead">A close community for students who want to explore widely, think deeply, and pursue a question of their own.</p>
    <p>The existing Primoris High School approach centers on a cohort of curious, driven peers, supported by faculty who encourage intellectual inquiry, creativity, and self-expression. Students connect demanding academic work with interests that can reach into research, professional practice, and university study.</p>

<section class="school-band" id="approach" aria-labelledby="approach-title">
  <p class="school-eyebrow">Our approach</p>
  <h2 id="approach-title">Depth, independence, and connection.</h2>
  <div class="school-grid">
    <article><h3>Learn for understanding</h3><p>Build a foundation that supports rigorous inquiry. Explain a method, question an assumption, and connect a result with the evidence behind it.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Take ownership</h3><p>Develop the habits that make independent work possible: planning, seeking feedback, revising, and communicating an idea clearly.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Work with others</h3><p>Use the classroom and studio as places to test ideas with peers and faculty. An individual interest becomes richer through conversation and collaboration.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="school-section" id="life" aria-labelledby="life-title">
  <p class="school-eyebrow">Life in High School</p>
  <h2 id="life-title">The people and the process matter.</h2>
  <p>High School is also the experience of working alongside peers, discussing a difficult idea, and learning to contribute to a community. The existing school description emphasizes collaboration, self-expression, and opportunities to make an impact.</p>
  <p class="school-draft-label">Illustrative week · Not a timetable</p>
  <dl class="school-week">
    <div><dt>Seminars &amp; lessons</dt><dd>Connect close reading, mathematical reasoning, languages, and scientific study through discussion and focused practice.</dd></div>
    <div><dt>Investigation &amp; making</dt><dd>Plan an experiment, build or revise a design, and use evidence to explain the result. Project time depends on the student's academic pathway.</dd></div>
    <div><dt>Choice &amp; expression</dt><dd>Explore an artistic, technical, or practical interest. Ask the school how electives, enrichment, and independent work fit into the current week.</dd></div>
    <div><dt>Reflection &amp; next steps</dt><dd>Review progress and prepare questions about academic planning, support, future study, and opportunities beyond the classroom.</dd></div>
  </dl>

<section class="school-section" aria-labelledby="questions-title">
  <p class="school-eyebrow">Questions families ask</p>
  <h2 id="questions-title">Before choosing a pathway.</h2>
  <div class="school-faq">
    <details><summary>How is the right track determined?</summary><p>A useful starting point is the student's readiness, interests, and goals. Ask Primoris how current placement decisions are made and which academic records, conversations, or classroom visits inform the process.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Can a student change tracks?</summary><p>Discuss changes with the school. Timing, prerequisites, graduation requirements, and the availability of courses or external placements may affect what is possible.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Which courses are required, and which are electives?</summary><p>The department pages describe areas of learning. Primoris can confirm the current required courses, elective options, and scheduling constraints for an individual track.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Are university courses and internships guaranteed?</summary><p>The existing track descriptions identify these as directions of study. Ask the school to confirm current arrangements, eligibility, supervision, credit, and availability for a particular student.</p></details>
  </div>
</section>
